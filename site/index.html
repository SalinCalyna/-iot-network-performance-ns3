V4 Investigation

Does adaptive routing solve the problem?

Sigmoid-based adaptive routing (V4) vs. Static, across 6 activation-screen configurations (N=50, medium traffic, 11 seeds each).

Research result
Routing activation ≠ performance improvement. The adaptive routing mechanism changed routing behaviour under selected conditions, but did not produce credible overall performance improvement relative to Static. This is treated as a valid scientific negative result, not a failed implementation.
ConfigurationRoute-tree change vs. StaticActivated?PDR (V4)PDR (Static)
The strongest-activation configuration (100% of seeds re-routed, mean hop count +27.9%) produced a PDR change of only +3.5%, not statistically credible (sign-test p=1.0), while delay worsened 21.6% and link utilisation worsened 42.8%.

Gateway Airtime vs. Network Size

High traffic condition

Primary measurable bottleneck
Gateway and first-hop congestion becomes dominant under near-saturated conditions. Gateway airtime reaches 0.86–0.89 at N≥75 under high traffic, and MAC-queue drops are 1.2–2.4× over-represented in the first-hop neighbourhood.
  • NO_ROUTE / ROUTE_ERROR: <2% of loss in every tested condition
  • Retry exhaustion: measured separately (see Retry Analysis)
Only 0–53% of actual packet loss is attributable to any single logged counter even at the most saturated cell — the bottleneck's location is well supported, not every lost packet has a known individual cause.

Improved OLSR PDR at high node density
The study identifies gateway and first-hop congestion as the dominant measurable bottleneck under near-saturated conditions and demonstrates that reducing offered traffic rate can effectively mitigate this bottleneck and improve PDR in OLSR networks at high node densities. However, the effectiveness of the intervention is protocol- and load-dependent, and a portion of packet loss remains unattributed by the current instrumentation.

Limitations

AODV
FlowMonitor PacketsSent-denominator artifact affects interpretation of PDR/PacketLoss — read as indicative, not exact.
OLSR
RoutingOverheadPackets = 0 is a metric-coverage limitation, not proof of zero control traffic.
Static
Some residual packet loss remains unattributed (64.65%–99.57% at Static/N=50, typically ≥92%).
Instrumentation
Not all packet-loss mechanisms are directly observable using the current counters. PHY RX drops are not directly equivalent to permanent packet loss.
V4
Routing adaptation did not produce measurable overall improvement, even at 100% activation.
