1. Overview

Research question: what is the main cause of packet loss in a multi-hop IoT network as network size and traffic load increase, and can the identified bottleneck be mitigated by reducing offered traffic rate?

Network gets larger→ Performance degrades→ V4 routing adaptation investigated→ Routing alone does not solve it→ Bottleneck probe: gateway/first-hop congestion→ Retry exhaustion: confirmed, secondary→ Offered-rate reduction tested→ OLSR N=75/100 improves→ Effect is protocol- and load-dependent
All reported research values are based on the frozen Final Report Data Package at analysis/final-report-data/ in the repository.

3. V4 Sigmoid-Based Routing Investigation

Sigmoid-based adaptive routing changed routing behaviour under selected configurations, but activation did not translate into a measurable performance improvement. This is a valid negative result, not a failed implementation.

ConfigurationRoute-tree change vs. StaticActivated?PDR (V4)PDR (Static)
The strongest-activation configuration (100% of seeds re-routed, mean hop count +27.9%) produced a PDR change of only +3.5%, which is not statistically credible (sign-test p=1.0), while delay worsened by 21.6% and link utilisation worsened by 42.8%.

4. Bottleneck Characterisation

176 runs (OLSR/Static × 4 node counts × 2 traffic levels, 11 seeds) instrumented per-node airtime, MAC-queue drops, and PHY RX drops to localise where loss occurs.

Figure 2 · Gateway airtime vs. node count (high traffic)

Evidence

  • Gateway airtime reaches 0.86–0.89 at N≥75 under high traffic — an empirical near-saturation reference for this topology.
  • MAC-queue drops are 1.2–2.4× over-represented in the first-hop neighbourhood.
  • NO_ROUTE / ROUTE_ERROR are negligible (<2% of loss) in every tested condition — routing failure is not the mechanism.
Only 0–53% of actual packet loss is attributable to any single logged counter even at the most saturated cell — the bottleneck's location is well supported, but not every lost packet has a known individual cause.

5. Retry Exhaustion

36-run validation directly measuring MAC-layer retry exhaustion (packets dropped after exceeding the retry limit).

ProtocolNTrafficRetry share of lossMAC-queue share of loss
Retry exhaustion is real (mean ≈6% of loss) but secondary to MAC-queue overflow in 10 of 12 cells, and concentrates farther from the gateway — the opposite spatial pattern from queue overflow.

6. Offered-Rate Intervention

72-run rate sweep (16→12→8→4 kbps) testing whether reducing offered traffic rate relieves the bottleneck identified above.

Figure 5 · OLSR PDR vs. offered rate

Figure 6 · OLSR gateway airtime vs. offered rate

At the deepest rate reduction (16→4 kbps): N=75 PDR +14.7 percentage points, N=100 PDR +25.2 percentage points, both monotonic across every tested seed. At N=100, delivered throughput rises at a reduced offered rate (+25.5% at 12 kbps) — the signature of recovering from congestion collapse, not simply sending less.

7. Static Routing — Negative Control (N=50)

Static routing at N=50 was tested under the same rate sweep, then diagnosed with a focused 12-run study.

Figure 7 · Static N=50 PDR vs. offered rate

Residual-loss diagnostic (all 12 runs)

RateSeedAttributable lossResidual loss
PDR does not improve (16 kbps is the highest-PDR condition of the four tested; change at the deepest cut is −7.6 percentage points). This is not a broken protocol: congestion was genuinely relieved, but the relieved mechanisms were never the dominant source of this condition's loss — residual (unattributed) loss ranges 64.65%–99.57% across all 12 runs.

9. Conclusions & Limitations

The study identifies gateway and first-hop congestion as the dominant measurable bottleneck under near-saturated conditions and demonstrates that reducing offered traffic rate can effectively mitigate this bottleneck and improve PDR in OLSR networks at high node densities. However, the effectiveness of the intervention is protocol- and load-dependent, and a portion of packet loss remains unattributed by the current instrumentation.

Limitations

  • AODV: FlowMonitor PacketsSent-denominator artifact — PDR/loss figures are indicative, not exact.
  • OLSR: RoutingOverheadPackets=0 in the V3 dataset is a metric-coverage limitation, not evidence of zero control traffic.
  • PHY RX drops are not directly equivalent to permanent packet loss.
  • Not every terminal packet fate is captured by the current instrumentation — the residual-loss finding is a measured gap, not a resolved mechanism.
  • The offered-rate intervention is a fixed-rate reduction, not an adaptive admission-control algorithm.
  • All findings are specific to the studied ns-3 topology, PHY/MAC configuration, and parameter set.
